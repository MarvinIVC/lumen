# What good looks like — English literature, *The Great Gatsby* ch. 1–3

The hardest fixture in the suite, and the constraint is simple: **every quotation in the output
must be one the student wrote down.** There are three of them, plus one attributed paraphrase.

- Devices are analysed **through those three quotations only**: "Reserving judgments is a matter
  of infinite hope" for the unreliable narrator; "he stretched out his arms toward the dark water
  in a curious way" for the green light as symbol; "In his blue gardens men and girls came and
  went like moths" for the simile and its colour imagery.
- **"Her voice is full of money" is kept as the student left it** — a paraphrase they were not
  sure of, attributed to "someone", and flagged as something to check with the text. It is not
  quoted as though it were exact, and no chapter or page number is invented for it.
- **No line numbers, no page numbers, no act/scene references.** None were supplied.
- Terms of art are defined: unreliable narrator, symbol vs motif, simile, imagery, juxtaposition.
- The East Egg / West Egg contrast becomes a table, because it is a comparison and the notes wrote
  it as one line.
- Theme tracing: the American Dream across the three chapters, with where it appears and how it
  develops — and an honest note that chapters 4 onwards are where it resolves.
- Two or three essay angles the student could actually argue **from the evidence they have**.
- The Eckleburg eyes get a "read through this lens" treatment rather than an assertion that they
  *are* God — the notes ask the question, and a good answer keeps it a question.
