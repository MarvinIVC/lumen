# What good looks like — History, origins of the Cold War

- **The date error is corrected and logged.** The Truman Doctrine is 1947, not 1948. One
  `correction`, marked inline, with a one-line why.
- **Long-term causes are separated from the trigger**, which is the analytical move the whole
  question turns on — and a mermaid causation diagram makes the separation visible.
- **A timeline** of 1945–49 with what each event changed, not just when it happened.
- **Each event carries its significance in one line.** Potsdam matters because the personnel and
  the bomb changed the balance, not because it happened in July.
- **The three historiographical schools become a table**: orthodox, revisionist, post-revisionist,
  each with what it claims and what evidence it leans on. The student already named all three;
  they are sharpened, not replaced.
- **No invented quotations, no invented statistics, no invented historians.** The student supplied
  no quotations at all, so the finished note contains none. "$13 billion" is theirs and stays;
  nothing similar is added. This is the check that matters most on this fixture.
- The Fulton speech is placed (March 1946, Churchill, out of office) because the notes name it
  without a date — `ai-added`, and no words are put in Churchill's mouth.
- An open question: the notes stop at the Berlin airlift, so does the lesson end there or continue
  to NATO?
