# Acids and bases (revision)

An acid is a substance with a pH below 7. A strong acid is one that is very concentrated.

pH = -log[OH-]

Neutralisation: acid + base -> salt + water. This is always exothermic and always gives pH 7.

Litmus goes red in acid and blue in base. Universal indicator gives an exact pH to 2 decimal
places.

Titration: put the acid in the burette and the alkali in the conical flask, add indicator, swirl
until it changes colour, that is the end point which is the same as the equivalence point.

Worked example: what mass of NaCl is in 0.25 mol?
M(NaCl) = 23 + 12 = 35
0.25 x 23.4 = 5.85 g

A catalyst speeds up a reaction by lowering the activation energy and shifting the equilibrium to
the right.
