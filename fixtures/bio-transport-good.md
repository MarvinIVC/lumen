# What good looks like — Biology, membrane transport

Short of the full gold treatment (`ap-chem-u1-gold.md` is the one worked all the way through);
this is the standard the eval judges against for a `stem-descriptive` lesson.

- **The osmosis definition is corrected and logged.** The notes have it backwards: water moves
  from *high* water potential to *low*. That is a `correction`, marked `ai-corrected` inline, with
  a one-line why — never a silent fix.
- **The sodium-potassium pump claim is corrected.** Three Na⁺ out and two K⁺ in makes the inside
  *more negative*, not more positive. This is the error the whole membrane-potential topic later
  depends on, so it earns a real explanation rather than a note.
- **Every term is defined**: water potential, solute potential, pressure potential, tonicity,
  turgid, lysis, facilitated diffusion, and the three endocytosis types the notes gesture at.
- **The comparison the notes started becomes a table**: passive vs active, on energy, direction
  relative to the gradient, protein involvement and examples.
- **The diagram the student promised themselves gets drawn** — a mermaid process diagram of the
  pump's cycle, marked `ai-added`, with a caption and alt text.
- **A plant-cell / animal-cell tonicity table**, because the notes give one half of each pair.
- Water potential appears as a three-part `formula` block: Ψ = Ψp + Ψs, every symbol with units
  (kPa), and a "use this when".
- Misconception blocks, distinct from the corrections: "osmosis needs energy", "active transport
  and facilitated diffusion both use proteins so they are the same thing".
- Nothing about aquaporins, cotransport or membrane potential in millivolts: that is the next
  lesson, and rubric item 5 forbids padding beyond the syllabus scope.
