# What good looks like — notes riddled with errors

Five errors, five logged corrections, five inline `ai-corrected` marks. None of them silent, and
none of them scolding: 06 §5 item 6 — "here is what to relearn", never "you were wrong".

1. **"A strong acid is one that is very concentrated."** Strong is about the *extent of
   dissociation*; concentrated is about *amount per volume*. The single most common confusion in
   this topic and worth a misconception block of its own as well as the correction.
2. **pH = −log[OH⁻]** → pH = −log[H₃O⁺]. The notes' version is pOH.
3. **"Neutralisation always gives pH 7."** Only for a strong acid with a strong base; a weak
   acid with a strong base lands above 7.
4. **The arithmetic.** M(NaCl) = 23 + 35.5 = 58.5 g·mol⁻¹, not 35, and 0.25 mol × 58.5 = **14.6 g**
   to three significant figures — not 5.85 g. The corrected worked example is finished with units
   at every step, a boxed answer, and the student's own attempt kept beside it.
5. **"A catalyst shifts the equilibrium to the right."** It lowers the activation energy of both
   directions equally and shifts nothing.
- "Universal indicator gives an exact pH to 2 decimal places" is corrected too, gently: it gives
  an approximate pH from a colour, which is why a pH meter exists.
- The titration description is *mostly right* and stays the student's, with one clarification: the
  end point and the equivalence point are close but not the same thing.
